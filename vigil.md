# Rosary Yoga — The Vigil

*A morning anchor. Five to ten minutes. The first practice of the day.*

Seven movements. At the foot of the bed, in whatever light the room has, before coffee, before the phone — the body lifted from sleep, the spine unrolled, the day not yet decided.

---

## Why this exists

The night has [`practice.md`](practice.md). The day has [`training.md`](training.md). Between them sits a small hinge: the first thing the body does after rising. Without it, the day begins where the inbox begins. With it, the day begins where the breath begins.

The Vigil is not a workout and not a stretch routine. It is the opening of the day's container — the morning parallel to the rosary anchor that closes it. Lauds answers Compline. The body wakes the way it slept: on the floor, in silence.

Calibrated for a small condo bedroom. Carpet is enough. Short enough for thirty years; long enough that the nervous system notices.

---

## How to use the Vigil

Two ways, the same as the night. Pick one.

1. **The Manual.** Read it twice. Run the seven movements from memory. The body learns the sequence inside a week. After that the phone stays face-down on the dresser.
2. **The App.** VIGIL speaks the cues. The app surfaces the day's modality variant. Use it the first weeks, on travel mornings, or any morning the body wants a thread to hold.

If you miss a morning, resume the next without ceremony.

---

## Lineage

The Vigil borrows from four traditions and invents nothing.

- **Surya Namaskar A** — the twelve-asana flow recorded in early-twentieth-century India, older than its recording. Breath synchronized to limb.
- **Ba Duan Jin** — the *Eight Pieces of Brocade*, a thousand-year-old standing qigong form practiced in Chinese parks before dawn. Slow loading of joints.
- **Monastic Lauds** — the dawn office in the Rule of St. Benedict, sung at 3 to 5 a.m., named for the *laudate* of Psalms 148–150. The body standing, the hour given before the world's claims.
- **Somatic orienting** — Peter Levine's protocol for the first minutes of waking. The eyes find the corners of the room; the nervous system steps from sleep on its own time rather than the alarm's.

The Vigil is none of these. It carries something from each and arranges them for a body that will lift, run, or sit at a desk for twelve hours.

## The seven movements

The order is fixed, every morning, every day. The body learns the cue.

| # | Movement | Breath | Time | What it does |
|---|----------|--------|------|--------------|
| I | Orient | 5 breaths, eyes open | 30–45 s | Wakes the senses; locates the body in the room |
| II | Supine Knees-to-Chest | 5 breaths | 45 s | Decompresses the low back |
| III | Reclined Side Stretch *(Banana)* | 4 breaths per side | 60 s | Opens the lateral chain |
| IV | Child's Pose to Cat-Cow | 6 breaths | 60 s | Mobilizes the spine in flexion and extension |
| V | Standing Forward Fold to Mountain | 4 breaths, rise on inhale | 45 s | Posterior chain; blood-pressure check |
| VI | Standing Brocade — Shoulder Opener | 6 breaths | 60 s | Opens chest; loads the postural chain |
| VII | Standing Still — The Hour | 8 breaths | 60–90 s | Threshold. The day is named. |

Total: approximately six minutes at canonical pace.

---

## Each movement in detail

### I · Orient

Sit on the edge of the bed or on the floor, feet flat. Let the eyes find the corners of the room — high left, high right, low left, low right. Then the window, the door, the floor. Five slow nasal breaths. The senses confirm: the room is the room, the body is the body. The nervous system needs to know it is safe before it is asked to move.

### II · Supine Knees-to-Chest *(Apanasana)*

Lie on the back. Draw both knees toward the chest. Hands on the shins. Five breaths. On each exhale, draw the knees a little closer. The low back lengthens against the floor. The pose that anchors the night's decades — the body recognizes it.

### III · Reclined Side Stretch *(Banana)*

Still on the back, arms overhead. Walk feet and upper body to one side until the body forms a long curve. Both shoulders and hips stay on the floor. Four breaths, then switch. The ribs compressed against the mattress open laterally. Pose IV of the opening pendant.

### IV · Child's Pose to Cat-Cow

Roll to the knees. Big toes touching, knees apart. Hips back to the heels — two breaths in Child's Pose, forehead to the floor. Come forward to all fours. Six breaths in cat-cow: inhale, belly drops, eyes lift; exhale, spine rounds, chin to chest. The breath leads.

### V · Standing Forward Fold to Mountain

Rise to standing. Feet hip-width. Fold forward from the hips, knees soft, head hanging heavy. Four breaths. On the fourth inhale, roll up one vertebra at a time. Arms rise overhead, palms meet, eyes lift. Exhale, hands to heart. The roll-up is slow on purpose — a blood-pressure check as much as a movement. If the head spins, pause halfway with hands on shins. Positions 2, 3, and 12 of Surya Namaskar A.

### VI · Standing Brocade — Shoulder Opener

Feet hip-width. Interlace the fingers behind the back, palms turning to face each other if they can. Lift the chest, draw the shoulder blades down and together, lift the hands slightly from the sacrum. Six breaths. The front of the shoulders lengthens; the upper back loads. The closest the Vigil comes to strength work. A simplified borrow from the *Eight Pieces of Brocade*.

### VII · Standing Still — The Hour

Feet hip-width, arms at the sides, palms forward. Eyes soft or closed. Weight even. Eight slow breaths. The Vigil's threshold — parallel to the closing Savasana of the night. The day is named, silently or aloud: *Monday. Joyful. Mobility.* The hands come to the heart, once, and the Vigil is done. Tadasana from yoga, *zhan zhuang* from qigong, the *Statio* of the Benedictine office.

## Modality variants

The seven-movement spine does not change. What changes is the emphasis.

- **Joyful (Mon, Sat) — Mobility.** The Vigil extends. After movement VII the operator slides directly into the day's longer mobility flow. No hard stop between them.
- **Sorrowful (Tue, Fri) — Endurance.** The Vigil softens. Movement VI drops to four breaths. Movement V runs one cycle, never two. The legs are protected for what comes later.
- **Glorious (Wed) — Strength.** The Vigil primes. Movement VI extends to eight breaths and adds one slow squat-to-stand at the close — descend through three breaths, rise through three. No reps. One squat. A small dose of load so the day's heavier work is not cold.
- **Luminous (Thu) — Skill.** The Vigil narrows. Movement VII is done eyes closed for the full eight breaths, weight shifting subtly from foot to foot — proprioceptive awareness for the skill ladder that follows. Each cat-cow in movement IV takes a full eight-count breath in each direction.
- **Sunday — Sanctum.** No modification. The Vigil runs as written, the day's only obligation alongside the logbook and the codex.

## The VIGIL voice

Liturgical, low, unhurried. Speaks in the register of monastic Lauds — the room cold, the candle lit, the voice not yet warmed. Never coaches. Never raises. Names what is.

> *The hour is yours. Begin.*

> *The body is on the floor. The breath has not yet been spent. Receive it.*

> *Orient. The room is the room. The body is the body. The day has not yet asked anything of you.*

> *In. Out. The spine unrolls. Nothing is required of the next movement except that it happen.*

> *Stand. Feet under the hips. Crown over the heels. The day will arrive in its own time.*

> *Eight breaths. Name the day. Then walk into it.*

> *The Vigil is done. The hand is yours.*

VIGIL never uses exclamation points and never says *let's*. VIGIL witnesses the body waking and names what is being woken.

## Integration

*Standing Still — The Hour* ends in Tadasana with hands at the heart. From there the operator moves to whatever the day asks: water, then coffee, then the work card on a training day; on Joyful days, directly into the extended mobility flow without breaking posture; on Sunday, to the logbook and the codex with the Vigil's stillness carried forward. The Vigil is the first decision of the day, already made — the body has shown up before the inbox or the calendar can. Breakfast and the day's work come after, in whatever order. The Vigil does not negotiate with them. It precedes them.

## Notes

**On the cue.** Within a week the body runs the sequence without thought. Within a month the Vigil is what waking is.

**On modifications.** The Vigil should never produce a sharp signal. If the standing fold makes the head spin, stay seated for it and rise at the end.

**On the relationship to the night.** Three of the seven movements — Knees-to-Chest, Banana, Child's Pose — are direct borrows from [`practice.md`](practice.md). The Vigil and the night practice are bookends of the same shape.

**On the long arc.** The Vigil will outlast the training program, the boss list, the app, the apartment. The goal is that this is still the first thing the body does in 2056.
